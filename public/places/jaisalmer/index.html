KNOW THE PLACE BEFORE THE JOURNEY

Destination overview

Jaisalmer is Rajasthan’s Golden City, close to the Thar Desert. Sandstone architecture, the fort and historic havelis give the city a character distinct from the desert experiences outside it. Rajasthan Tourism also highlights nearby cultural sites, fossil landscapes and dune excursions.

Sam and Khuri are among the areas associated with camel safaris and desert stays. The city and dunes offer different experiences: walking among historic buildings is not the same as an excursion or camp evening. Plan them separately and compare what an operator actually includes before choosing a package.

Experiences to explore
  • Jaisalmer Fort — A living heritage setting: remember that these lanes are also residential.
  • Patwon ki Haveli — Architecture and craftsmanship; confirm the ticket applicable to each section.
  • Gadisar Lake — A slower waterfront stop; verify activities and schedules locally.
  • Sam or Khuri desert excursion — Compare location, group size and operator practices before committing.
Choose your base
  • Old city — atmosphere and walking access; check stairs and vehicle access.
  • Outside the fort — easier pickups and a broader choice of properties.
  • Desert camp — assess sanitation, noise, transfers and what is actually included.
Food & the local table

Look for Rajasthani thalis and regional vegetarian dishes. Confirm spice levels and water arrangements for desert transfers.

A flexible starter itinerary
  1. Old-city lanes and a haveli visit, with a relaxed evening.
  2. Fort exploration followed by a separately planned desert excursion.
  3. A slower lake stop, craft browsing and onward travel.
Access, etiquette & packing

Access: Fort streets, stairs and sand create mobility barriers. Ask about step-free accommodation, transport drop-off points and camp toilets.

Local etiquette: Ask permission for portraits and private interiors. Choose considerate wildlife and animal-tourism practices.

Bring along: Sun protection, closed footwear for sand, water and layers for changing desert temperatures.
